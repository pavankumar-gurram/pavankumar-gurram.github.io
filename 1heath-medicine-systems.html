How Many People Use Them

Why the Doctor Needs to Know

Most AYUSH use is not a problem. The problem is that the doctor does not know about it, so cannot check it against what they are about to prescribe. Three kinds of risk matter:

  • Interactions. Some herbs change how modern drugs act; blood thinners and diabetes medicines are the classic examples.
  • Organ injury. In one Indian case series, single-ingredient ashwagandha products caused liver injury in 8 patients; 3 died, all of whom had underlying liver disease[2].
  • Hidden ingredients. Some preparations contain metals or undeclared drugs. 1Heath flags preparations whose official formula lists metals or minerals.

How 1Heath Handles It

Phase 1: "Also Taking"

For every patient, a list of everything they take from every system, in their own words: "amma ichina kashayam", "Thyronorm", "sugar-ki desi dawa". Where the official pharmacopoeia lists ingredients, they are shown. Known herb–drug interactions show as a warning to the doctor, never as advice to the patient.

Phase 2: AYUSH Doctors and Pharmacies

The same Doctor app, with case fields from the Ministry of AYUSH's case-record format for Ayurvedic doctors (Ashtavidha Pariksha, Dashavidha Pariksha, Samprapti), and remedy and potency for homeopathic doctors. Every field is optional and filled only when said.

Catalogue and Coding

The demo catalogue holds 1,027 Ayurvedic formulations from the Ayurvedic Formulary of India and the AYUSH essential list, and 1,117 homeopathic remedies from the Homoeopathic Pharmacopoeia of India. For a pilot, 1Heath will move to national standards: the SNOMED CT India AYUSH Extension maintained by NRCeS[3], NAMASTE terms, and the WHO ICD-11 traditional medicine chapter, which covers Ayurveda, Siddha and Unani but not yet homeopathy.

Cross-System Prescribing

A doctor prescribing outside their own system sees a warning to acknowledge, not a block, because the law on cross-system practice varies and the decision belongs to the doctor.

Neutrality Rules

  • 1

    1Heath never ranks or endorses a system of medicine.

  • 2

    It never tells a patient to stop or start a medicine; only the doctor does.

  • 3

    It shows evidence and official formulae, and labels what is unknown as unknown.
