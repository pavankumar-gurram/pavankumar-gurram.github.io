Key Points
  • Impact is modelled with one formula: harm avoided = harm that would happen × share of visits where the history is on record × how much having the history reduces errors.
  • The size of the reduction comes from trials of the closest existing tools: electronic prescribing cut prescribing errors by 48%[1]; pharmacist-checked medicine lists cut drug-harm hospital revisits by 67%[2].
  • For every 1,000 doctors using 1Heath, by year three: about 4,000 history-related medication incidents and about 170 severe harms avoided each year, and a few deaths (about 4).
  • If every Indian's history were available at every visit, the same evidence suggests about 3,000 hospital deaths and about 74,000 severe harms avoided every year.
  • Most of the value is not in a single number: legible signed prescriptions, generic names, allergy checks at the pharmacy and a record that follows the family.

The Impact Formula

In one sentence: the harm 1Heath prevents = the harm that would have happened × how often the patient's history is already in 1Heath × how much having that history cuts errors.

Consultations per doctor a year9,00030 a day for 300 days; our planning assumption for a small clinic
Visits per patient a year3Our assumption; gives 3,000 patients per doctor
Coverage30% → 50% → 70%Years one to three. Our assumption: coverage grows as patients return and families join
Effect28% to 67%, likely 48%Electronic prescribing reduced prescribing errors by 48%[1]. Medicine reconciliation reduced emergency visits by 28% and drug-harm hospital revisits by 67%[2]

These trials were in hospitals in high-income countries. 1Heath's own effect must be measured in its pilot; until then these are borrowed estimates.

How We Will Measure the Real Impact

Borrowed effect sizes are a starting point. The pilot will measure 1Heath's own:

  • 1

    Allergy and interaction alerts that changed a prescription, counted from the signed record, per 1,000 consultations.

  • 2

    Coverage: the share of consultations where the patient already had a record, month by month.

  • 3

    Prescription completeness: strength, dose, frequency, duration and generic name present, against a sample of handwritten slips from the same clinics before 1Heath.

  • 4

    Independent review: a sample of flagged and unflagged consultations reviewed by a clinician who is not part of the team.
