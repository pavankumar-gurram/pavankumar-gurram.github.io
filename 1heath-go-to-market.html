Key Points
  • Doctors first, then patients: every signed prescription brings a patient, and every patient brings a family. No marketing to patients.
  • First users: general physicians, paediatricians and gynaecologists in small clinics in one Telugu-speaking metro, reached doctor to doctor and through a 15-minute demo on their own phone.
  • Where: medical colleges, medical association branches and specialty societies, then the clinic pharmacies and chemists those doctors already work with.
  • Targets: 100 doctors in year one, 1,000 in year two, 4,000 in year three, at under ₹20,000 per active doctor.
  • Never: payments or gifts to doctors, influence on prescribing, or sale of patient data.
  • The pilot must show that doctors keep using it after four weeks.

Who Adopts First

SpecialistsLong histories and repeat prescriptionsAfter the pilot
Ayurvedic and homeopathic doctorsLittle clinic software is built for them; their own case fieldsPhase 2
Clinic pharmaciesPrescriptions arrive legible and completeWith their doctors
Patients and familiesTheir own record and an emergency cardArrive with every signed prescription

Geography: one Telugu-speaking metro first, where the team can support every clinic in person and the models are strongest, then other metros, then district towns. The market is large: 13,88,185 registered allopathic doctors and 7,51,768 AYUSH practitioners[1].

Doctors First, Then Patients

1Heath does not buy patients. The record grows the way care already flows: a doctor signs a prescription, and everyone downstream of that prescription is reached through it.

StepHow patients joinWhat they get
At the deskABHA linked or created with consent; India already has 98.9 crore ABHA accounts[2]A record that any ABDM-connected provider can request with their consent
After the visitA link or QR code on the prescription opens the visit summaryMedicines, timings and instructions in their own language
At homeThey add family members and home medicines from every systemReminders, family history and an emergency card
At the next doctorThey share the record by consent, even with a doctor not using 1HeathA doctor who knows their allergies and medicines

The Funnel, in Numbers

From demo to active doctor (targets)

  1. Doctors shown a 15-minute demo5,000
  2. 1 in 3 start a free trial1,667
  3. 6 in 10 of those still use it after four weeks1,000 doctors

The cost of winning each active doctor (all sales and support spending divided by active doctors) should stay under ₹20,000.

These ratios are targets until the pilot measures them. If trials convert at 1 in 5 instead of 1 in 3, the same 1,000 doctors need about 8,300 demos, and the cost per doctor rises accordingly; the business model shows what that does to payback.

Channels

  • Medical colleges: live demos at teaching hospitals in the launch city, then nationally.
  • Associations and CMEs: short demos at Indian Medical Association branches and specialty meetings.
  • Doctors' groups: two-minute videos in Telugu, Hindi and English, showing a real consultation and the signed prescription.
  • Launch: a modest event at a medical college in the launch city.

What We Tell Doctors, and What We Do Not

We sayWe do not say
It removes typing from your consultation"It saves X minutes", until the pilot measures it. Evidence from other scribes shows modest time savings[3]
It never fills in a dose you did not say"It is 100% accurate"
You see your own words next to every line"You do not need to check it"
It records what your patient takes from every systemAnything that ranks one system above another

Rules That Do Not Bend

  • 1

    Doctors are never paid, gifted or rewarded for what they prescribe.

  • 2

    Patients can use any pharmacy, and generic names are always printed.

  • 3

    No pharmaceutical sponsorship and no advertising to patients.

  • 4

    Patient data is never sold.

  • 5

    Safety features are never part of a paid tier.

What the Pilot Must Prove

QuestionMeasureTarget
Do doctors keep using it?Doctors still active after four weeks6 in 10
Is it safe on real voices?Invented medicines; unflagged dose errors0
Is it quick enough?Lines needing confirmation per consultation3 or fewer
Do clinics value it?Clinics paying after the free month3 in 10
Can we reach doctors efficiently?Cost to win one active doctorunder ₹20,000

Every week we track demos given, installs, first consultations completed, doctors still active after four weeks, and cost per active doctor. These are targets; this page will show the real numbers once the pilot runs.

Risks and Answers

RiskAnswer
Doctors try it and stopThe first thing the pilot measures; demos on their own phone; advisors as first users
Established scribes move fasterCompete on verification, every system of medicine and the record after the visit, not on transcription alone
Accuracy is lower on real voicesNo accuracy claims until measured; the doctor confirms everything uncertain
A safety incident damages trustThe doctor signs everything; blocking checks; frozen test sets; claims limited to what is measured
Regulation changesClassification opinion from CDSCO and a legal opinion before launch[4]
