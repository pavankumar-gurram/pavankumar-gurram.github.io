How Medicines and Money Move in India's Pharma Industry

From factory to clinic: the distribution chain and its margins, how companies reach doctors and the rules that govern it, price control, and how new medicines are funded.

Key Points
  • Indians bought about ₹2.41 lakh crore of medicines in 2025[1], mostly branded generics: old molecules sold under many brand names, nearly 60,000 of them[2].
  • Because the molecules are the same, companies compete for the doctor's choice of brand. That is why medical representatives, samples and, at times, illegal freebies exist.
  • Medicines travel maker → carrying-and-forwarding agent → wholesaler → retailer, with roughly 3%, 10% and 20% margins on a ₹100 medicine[3].
  • Marketing rules (UCPMP 2024) ban gifts, travel and cash to doctors but are voluntary[4]; the Supreme Court has held such freebies illegal[5].
  • Indian companies spend about 6% of revenue on research[6]; India's first home-grown antibiotic took 14 years and ₹500 crore[7].

What India Buys

₹2.41 lakh crDomestic medicine sales in 2025, up 8.1%Pharmarack[1]
3,000+Pharmaceutical companies, with 10,500 manufacturing unitsGovernment factsheet[2]
12.4 lakhChemists and drug distributors represented by their national bodyAIOCD[8]

The best-selling brands by annual sales in 2025 were Augmentin (an antibiotic) and Glycomet GP (a diabetes combination); by December, the new weight-loss and diabetes injection Mounjaro had moved to first place in monthly sales[1]. Most medicines sold are branded generics: off-patent molecules made by many companies and sold under brand names.

How a Medicine Reaches the Patient

  • Price control. Medicines on the National List of Essential Medicines are price-controlled under the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013. As of December 2025, ceiling prices were fixed for 935 formulations; re-fixing prices under the 2022 list cut them by about 17% on average, saving patients about ₹3,802 crore a year[9].
  • Trade margins. Margins on medicines outside price control are largely unregulated. A 2019 pilot capped trade margins on 42 cancer medicines; a parliamentary committee in December 2025 criticised a five-year delay in extending it[10].
  • Doctors who dispense. A registered doctor may supply medicines to their own patients without a shop licence, provided they do not run a shop open to the public[11]. This is the legal basis on which clinic dispensaries operate.
  • Generic stores. 18,646 Jan Aushadhi stores sell 2,110 generic medicines at 50 to 80% below brand prices[12].
  • Online pharmacies. Rules for e-pharmacies are still proposals, not law. Chemists called a nationwide strike for 20 May 2026 against unregulated online sales and deep discounting[8].

How Pharma Companies Reach Doctors

When many companies sell the same molecule, the doctor's pen decides who sells. Companies employ large field forces of medical representatives who visit clinics, explain products and leave samples. The legitimate part is information. The illegitimate part is inducement.

RuleWhat UCPMP 2024 says[4]
GiftsNo gift for the personal benefit of a healthcare professional or their family
Travel and hospitalityNo paid travel, hotel stays or expensive meals, except for speakers at medical education events
CashNo cash or monetary grant under any pretext
Free samplesOnly to doctors qualified to prescribe them; no more than 12 sample packs per drug per doctor per year; total value under 2% of the company's domestic sales
Medical education eventsNo events abroad; spending published on the company's website
EnforcementEthics committees in industry associations; appeals to a committee under the Secretary, Department of Pharmaceuticals. The code is voluntary
  • The Supreme Court on freebies. In Apex Laboratories (February 2022), the Court held that gifts to doctors are prohibited by the medical ethics regulations, so companies cannot deduct their cost as a business expense[5].
  • The Dolo-650 allegation. In August 2022, the federation of medical representatives told the Supreme Court that tax authorities had accused the maker of Dolo-650 of distributing ₹1,000 crore of freebies to doctors, and asked for the marketing code to be made law[13]. The Court called it "a serious issue".

How New Medicines Are Funded

5.9%Average research spend of India's ten largest listed drug makers, as a share of revenue (highest: Dr. Reddy's, 8.2%)GlobalData, FY 2023-24[6]
$985 mMedian research and development cost of a new medicine reaching the US market, 2009 to 2018 (mean $1.3 billion)JAMA, 2020[14]
14 yearsAnd ₹500 crore to develop nafithromycin, India's first indigenously developed antibiotic, for drug-resistant pneumonia; the government's BIRAC funded ₹8 crore of its final trialsDown To Earth, 2024[7]
₹5,000 crThe government's PRIP scheme (2023-24 to 2029-30): ₹700 crore for research centres at NIPERs and ₹4,200 crore for company researchDepartment of Pharmaceuticals[15]

Indian companies generally spend 5 to 10% of revenue on research[6]; Sun Pharma, for example, spent ₹3,178 crore in 2023-24[6]. The government's scheme supports six domains: new drugs, complex generics, biosimilars, cell and gene therapies, high-value patented or near-expiry drugs, and advanced medical devices, and co-funds up to 35% of a late-stage project's cost[15].
