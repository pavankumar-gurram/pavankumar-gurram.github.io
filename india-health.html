Key Points
  • Non-communicable diseases cause 56.7% of deaths; heart disease alone causes 31%[1].
  • 10.1 crore Indians have diabetes and 31.5 crore have high blood pressure[2].
  • Over 15 lakh new cancer cases were estimated in 2024[3]; road crashes kill about 20 people an hour[4].
  • Patients pay about half of current health spending out of their own pockets[5].

The digital rails are large: 127.5 crore health records are linked to ABHA IDs, and 6.09 lakh facilities and 13.98 lakh professionals are verified[7]. What is still thin is the record written at the point of care, in a two-minute consultation[8].

Life and Death

72.2 yearsLife expectancy at birth, 2024World Bank[9]
25Infant deaths per 1,000 live births in 2023, down from 40 in 2013SRS 2023[10]
28Deaths before age five per 1,000 live births in 2023, down from 48 in 2015UN IGME[11]

Progress is real but uneven. Infant mortality is 5 per 1,000 in Kerala and 37 in Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh and Uttar Pradesh; it is 28 in rural India against 18 in cities[10]. Newborn deaths fell from 28 to 17 per 1,000 between 2015 and 2023[11].

How Many Indians Die, and of What

about 89 lakhDeaths in India in 2023: 86.6 lakh were registered, and about 97 in 100 deaths are registeredCRS 2023[12][13]; our estimate
about 24,000Deaths every day, about one every 3 to 4 secondsour estimate from the yearly total
22%Share of registered deaths whose cause was certified by a doctor; for the rest, the cause comes from surveysMCCD 2023[14]

The total matches the government's own sample survey, which counts 6.3 deaths a year for every 1,000 people[15]. To see what these deaths are caused by, the Sample Registration System sends trained surveyors to households in a fixed sample of villages and towns to ask about each death, and doctors assign the cause[1]. Air pollution is a risk, not a separate cause. About 21 lakh deaths in India in 2021 were linked to polluted air, including 1.69 lakh children under five[26]. Most are counted above as heart, lung or infection deaths.

What the gaps say. Snakebite deaths are counted as about 10,000 by the police and about 58,000 by careful household study. Dengue deaths are 297 in one government count and 2,340 in another. When most deaths happen without a doctor's certificate, and the medical history of the person is not written down anywhere, the country cannot see what is killing its people. A signed record of every visit is part of the fix.

In men, lip and oral cavity cancer (about 15.6% of new cases) and lung cancer (about 8.5%) lead[28]. Survival depends heavily on early detection, which depends on someone noticing a pattern across visits. More on how other countries achieve higher survival is on the comparison page.

Road Accidents and Emergencies

4,80,583Road accidents in 2023, up 4.2% on 2022MoRTH[30][4]
1,72,890People killed, about 20 every hourMoRTH[4]
4,62,825People injuredMoRTH[4]

Since May 2025, road accident victims anywhere in India can get cashless treatment of up to ₹1.5 lakh for seven days at designated hospitals[31]. Paying for the first week solves one problem; knowing the victim's blood group, allergies and medicines is another. That is what an emergency card is for.

What Illness Costs Families

5.5 croreIndians pushed into poverty by health spending in a single year; 3.8 crore by medicines alonePHFI study in The BMJ, 2018[32]
80%+Indians with no health expenditure coverage in 2017–18NSS 75th round[33]
80%Rural families who paid for a hospital stay from savings; 13% borrowedNSS 75th round[33]

Medicines are the largest single cause of impoverishment[32]. A record that prevents a duplicate prescription or a repeated test saves money for the family that pays. Insurance has expanded since 2017–18, so the coverage figure is a baseline, not today's number.

Who Pays, and Where People Go First

43.52 croreAyushman Bharat (PM-JAY) cards issued, covering up to ₹5 lakh of hospital care per family a yearRajya Sabha, February 2026[34]
11.69 croreHospital admissions authorised under PM-JAY so far, worth ₹1.73 lakh crore, at 36,229 hospitals (19,483 public, 16,746 private)Rajya Sabha, February 2026[34]
1.78 lakhAyushman Arogya Mandirs, the government's local primary health centres, open as of July 2025Lok Sabha[35]
818Medical colleges, up from 387 in 2014; MBBS seats rose from 51,348 to 1,28,875Rajya Sabha, December 2025[36]

PM-JAY pays for hospital stays, not for outpatient visits or most medicines, which is where Indian families spend most of their own money[32]. In 2024 the scheme was opened to everyone aged 70 and over; 1.14 crore of these cards had been issued by February 2026[34]. The first point of contact for most illnesses remains a small private clinic or a local health centre, the setting 1Heath is built for.

How each state adds to PM-JAY, and how to use a scheme step by step, is on the government health schemes page.

Children's Nutrition

29.3%Children under five who are stunted, too short for their age from long-term undernutrition; down from 35.5% in 2019–21NFHS-6, 2023–24[37]
31.8%Children under five who are underweight; 19% are wasted, too thin for their heightNFHS-6, 2023–24[37]

In the same survey, adults moved the other way: 30.7% of women and 27.3% of men are now overweight or obese, up from 24% and 22.9%[37]. India carries both problems at once. Why, and what has to change, is on the page why Indians fall ill. A child's weight and growth, recorded at every visit, are what a paediatrician needs to dose medicines safely and to notice faltering growth early.

Mothers and Inherited Disease

~87Maternal deaths per lakh live birthsSRS special bulletin, 2022–24[38]
10,000–15,000Children born with thalassaemia major each year; 1 to 1.5 lakh living with itUnion Health Minister, 2020[39]

A woman arriving at night in labour may not be able to give her blood group, a previous caesarean or her blood pressure history. Thalassaemia major is preventable when carriers know their status before having children, which requires family records that link, with consent.

Why are these diseases so common in India, and what do countries with lower rates do differently? See India's biggest diseases.

What This Means for Medical Records

  • Long-term disease dominates. Heart disease, diabetes and high blood pressure need years of medicines from several doctors. A record that follows the patient prevents duplicates and omissions.
  • Patients pay. With about half of current health spending out of pocket[5], every repeated test and every avoidable admission is paid by a family.
  • Emergencies need four facts fast. Blood group, allergies, current medicines, long-term conditions.
  • Families share risk. Inherited conditions need linked family records, used with consent and counselling.
